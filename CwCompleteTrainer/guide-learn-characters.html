3I Want to Learn Morse Code Characters

You're starting from zero, or picking up new characters one at a time. The goal is instant recognition of each character's sound — not counting dits and dahs. See Philosophy for why the app is built around this approach.

Where to Go

Open Character Training from the launcher. Leave it in Koch mode — you start with just two characters (K and M) and unlock one new character at a time. Stay at a level until you can copy at least 90% comfortably, then tap the next chip to advance.

Selecting Specific Characters

Koch isn't the only mode. The mode selector at the top of the Character Training screen offers two alternatives if you want more control over which characters you drill:

  • Selected Characters — tap individual chips to pick exactly which characters to practise, regardless of Koch order. Useful for a targeted drill on specific characters you keep getting wrong.
  • Koch with Exclusions — keeps the normal Koch progression but skips individually excluded characters. Useful if you want to defer a character you find especially hard without losing your place in the overall order.

See Character Training for the full reference on all three modes.

Settings to Review

  • Speed (WPM) — keep it at a comfortable full-character speed (default 25). The Koch method deliberately trains characters at full target speed from day one; slowing individual characters down encourages counting instead of recognition.
  • Farnsworth WPM — the app ships with Farnsworth on by default (20 WPM). For pure single-character drilling, many learners prefer turning it off: each character is already isolated by the exercise format, so the extra inter-character spacing adds less value here than it does for words and sentences.
  • Enable TTS — turn it on with Normal pronunciation mode, so each character's name is spoken back to you right after its Morse plays. This confirms what you heard immediately.
  • Text display mode — set to At text end instead of the default Progressive. Combined with TTS on, this forces you to recognise each character by ear alone: nothing on screen gives it away in advance, and TTS still confirms what you heard afterward.
The recommended approach: stay at a Koch level until characters are instant, then advance one at a time. Jumping ahead before a level is solid tends to slow overall progress rather than speed it up.

Ready for a timed drill? Try the Character Match Quick Game — it uses your current Koch level automatically.
