7I Want to Read a Book in CW

Not a drill — you want to sit back and enjoy a long piece of text, played straight through in its original order, the way you'd read a book or a long article.

The single best way to build true Instant Character Recognition (ICR) with a book: turn the text display off entirely and just listen to the CW — no reading along, no TTS. If you can follow the story purely by ear, your ICR is solid.

Where to Go

Open Text & QSO Training from the launcher and use From File. Tap Pick a .txt file, choose your book or story from your device's Downloads folder, then choose Save as Story and give it a title. Long files are automatically split into segments of roughly 5,000 characters each, and My Stories tracks how far you've progressed in each segment — including a Resume chip so you can pick up exactly where you left off.

Settings to Review

  • Exercise length doesn't apply to story segments — the General → Exercise length setting is skipped entirely for story playback, so there's nothing to configure here; the automatic ~5,000-character segmentation already keeps each session a manageable size.
  • HCB (Head Copy Booster) — on by default, HCB plays some exercises faster first and then drops to a slower speed before revealing text, which suits active drilling more than relaxed reading. Consider turning it off for a book.
  • Text display mode — personal preference here: Full text at start if you're happy following along visually while you listen, or Chars as played / At text end if you'd rather practise pure listening comprehension on real prose. For the most demanding version — no visual cue at all, forcing you to follow the story by ear — hide the display this way and turn on TTS; you lose the relaxed-reading feel, but gain a genuine long-form listening drill.
  • Speed (WPM) / Farnsworth — set to whatever steady-state speed you can comfortably follow for an extended session; there's no need to push speed the way you would in a drill.
This is different from Word Training → From File, which shuffles a word list into random order — good for vocabulary drilling, not for reading a book. Use Text & QSO Training → From File whenever you want the original sequence preserved.
