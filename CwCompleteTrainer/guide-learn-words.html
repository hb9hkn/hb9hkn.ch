4I Know the Characters, I Want to Learn Words

You can recognise individual characters reliably and are ready for the next step: hearing full words as a unit instead of character-by-character.

Where to Go

Open Word Training from the launcher and use Common Words. The app loads real English words up to your configured length, shuffles them, and plays each one the configured number of times.

Settings to Review

  • Max word length (on the Word Training screen itself) — start short, 3–4 characters, and raise it as words start feeling instant. Default is 5.
  • Repeat count (Word Training screen, 1×/2×/3×) — set to 2× or 3× while a length is new, drop to 1× once words are easy.
  • Farnsworth WPM — unlike single-character drilling, keep Farnsworth on here (default 20 WPM). Words are harder to parse than isolated characters, and the extra gap gives you time to assemble the sounds into a word before the next one starts.
  • Auto Speed — Enable — a good fit for this kind of continuous drilling: it's on by default and gradually raises your WPM every 500 characters played, with no manual adjustment needed.
  • Enable TTS — keep it on for spoken confirmation after each word.
  • Text display mode — set to At text end so the word isn't shown on screen while it plays. With TTS on you'll still hear it confirmed afterward — you're forced to recognise the word by ear first, not read it.
Word Training also has HAM Abbreviations (QRM, QRN, QSO, 73, RST reports, …) and Call Signs — useful once basic vocabulary is solid and you're preparing for real on-air copy. Both use the same repeat-count and TTS settings as Common Words.

Ready for a timed drill? Try the Word / Callsign Match Quick Game from Quick Games.
